Weight tying and token embeddings

Weight Tying Biases Token Embeddings Towards the Unembedding Space

A. Lopardo, A. Harish, C. Arnett, A. Gupta

ACL 2026

Many language models tie weights, using one matrix for both the input embeddings and the output scores. Tying saves parameters, but many recent large models have reverted to distinct matrices. We ran a mechanistic study of the impact of weight tying and why it seems to hurt at scale. Comparing tied and untied models (a matched OLMo-1B pair plus GPT-Neo, Pythia, and Qwen3) shows the tied matrix resembles an untied output matrix far more than an input embedding, so it is shaped mainly for next-token prediction.

The cause is in training: early on, the output-side gradient dominates the input-side gradient and pulls the shared matrix toward the output role. A tuned lens analysis shows the cost, since early layers of tied models are less compatible with the final output space and contribute less to the residual stream. Scaling up the input-side gradients pulls the matrix back toward input-like structure, which gives causal evidence for the imbalance.

A brain-routed mixture-of-blocks language model

Berkeley AI ResearchPaper in preparation

While Mixture-of-experts models route tokens to different experts, nothing guarantees those experts specialize in some interpretable idea. We identify whether the brain can supply that structure.

We split a pretrained language model into blocks and route between them using clusters of curated brain data, so each block corresponds to a group of brain responses. We then test whether the brain's routing can be recovered and if the blocks maintain their specialization.

Cross-lingual fairness in LLM moral reasoning

Cross-lingual Fairness Drift in LLM Moral Reasoning

E. Xie, A. Chang-Lee, A. Harish, A. Vaidheeswaran

ICLR CAO Workshop 2026

A behavioral study of how language models reason about moral dilemmas in English, Spanish, Korean, and Mandarin. We score responses on a multi-part rubric and measure how much the models' reasoning shifts across languages, which makes language a useful axis for fairness audits.
