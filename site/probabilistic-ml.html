The Critical Impact of Non-Stationarity on Bayesian Decision Making for Self-Driving Labs

M. Noack, A. Gongora, A. Harish

Self-driving labs use Gaussian processes to decide which experiment to run next. The usual choice, a stationary kernel such as Matérn, assumes the function behaves the same way everywhere. Real measurements don't: a signal can be smooth in one region and change quickly in another.

I develop non-stationary kernels (Gibbs, Paciorek–Schervish, and deep kernels) that learn location-dependent variance and length scales from the data. The paper looks at how stationary kernels miscalibrate uncertainty, and how that leads the acquisition function to pick poor next experiments, across chirp signals, topography, and X-ray scattering data.
